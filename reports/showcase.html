Wind, Price & Storage in I-SEM

Two years of Irish day-ahead prices; a forecast built only from what a trader knows at the 11:00 gate closure; and what that forecast is worth to a 1 MW / 2 MWh battery. Everything runs from public data, through a DuckDB/SQL layer, in one command.

The market

Gas sets the level. Wind sets the shape.

Monthly I-SEM prices move almost one-for-one with GB (correlation ), a market where gas sets the price in most hours. Within a month, wind decides which hours are cheap. Each extra 10 points of wind penetration lowers the price by about  EUR/MWh.

The battery

A forecast worth per MW a year

Each trading day the schedule is optimised as a mixed-integer programme: power, SoC 5–95%, 88% round-trip efficiency, one cycle a day, no simultaneous charge and discharge. It is then settled at actual prices. The baseline is a fixed seasonal schedule learned from Year 1.

What these numbers are not

Read before quoting
  • DAM only. No DS3 system services, capacity, intraday or balancing revenue, which make up much of an Irish battery's income.
  • No degradation cost, network charges or imbalance exposure. The battery is assumed to be a price-taker.
  • Concentrated. The best 10% of days earn of perfect-foresight revenue.
Data integrity

The day the prices moved by a day

  1. An impossible chart. The spread history showed a month, July 2023, with a median daily spread of zero. No real day-ahead market does that.
  2. The public file had 54 “flat” days, each with one price for all 24 hours.
  3. Checking against the source. 363 official SEMOpx result files matched the public series only after shifting it by −24 h, and then matched exactly in every hour.
  4. Two more independent checks. The corrected price lines up best with same-hour net load, and its cheap days fall on Saturday and Sunday, not Sunday and Monday.
  5. Fixed and logged. Timestamps were relabelled, flat days repaired from official files or excluded, and every hour tagged with its source.

Blocked sources, not bypassed

EirGrid's file pages refused scripts (HTTP 403) and the dashboard rate-limited hard, so neither was worked around. Wind and load came from ENTSO-E via Energy-Charts instead, validated against EirGrid's own exports (correlation 0.998).

A wrong guess, corrected in the log

One dashboard feed was first judged UTC from a single DST edge case. Direct comparison with an independent series showed it was local time. The decision log records both the error and the fix.

Deterministic by design

Results drifted between runs until two causes were found: model threading, and unordered SQL rows feeding row-sampled training. Now two runs give identical numbers.
